# Creative production rules

## Truth and rights
- UK English, £ by default, no em dashes on screen. Claims supported by brand facts only. Never invent prices, capabilities, performance, availability, customer quotes or results. Respect banned claims/colours.
- Demonstration data must be masked and identified as illustrative when otherwise mistaken for real customer evidence. Omit unnecessary sample data. Never present fictional testimonials or results as genuine.
- Only supplied/cleared media and commercially licensed sound. Website retrieval grants no reuse rights. Never copy reference wording, signature scenes, music or identity.
- Approved factual competitor comparisons only; no borrowed logos. No fabricated people/logos, portrait lip-sync, misleading actual property/food/product depictions or unsupported regulated advice.

## Creative direction
- Start with audience, occasion, objective, barrier, insight and evidence. Choose the story for the brief, not a universal SaaS formula.
- One meaningful argument. Hook may be action, image, question, observation or reveal, not necessarily a headline/pain point.
- Demonstration, human observation, sensory ritual, comparison, metaphor, editorial type and guided tour are options, not compulsory templates.
- Rich assets merit shot selection, trimming and continuity. Sparse assets merit deliberate typography, diagrams, authorised stills or screenshots. Never invent evidence or prescribe interface cards to unrelated sectors.
- Focal hierarchy and readable duration for each moment. Composition, type, motion, imagery, sound and pacing need a meaning. Purposeful repetition can establish rhythm.
- Resolve the promise with one suitable next step. Continuing action, loop, invitation, payoff or held frame can work. A pill button and fixed end-card hold are optional.
- Compare argument, hook, story mechanism, composition, motion, imagery, pacing and ending against current batch and scoped recent history. New colours/nouns are insufficient. Preserve brand continuity without cloning layouts.

## Render, media and accessibility
- Intentional content from frame 1, no accidental blank lead-in. Exact ordered dimensions/duration.
- Supplied safe zones and caption reservation; readable phone-sized text and contrast. Whole words, no character scrambles.
- Real logos only, never redraw. Exact supplied file paths and inspected media treatments. No invented assets or remote runtime dependencies.
- New self-contained editable composition per asset. Deterministic seekable GSAP; no clocks, randomness, timers, autoplay or CSS animation loops.
- Report conflicts before building. A technical pass is not creative approval. Failed/missing review and exhausted repairs remain unresolved.

## Audio and review
- Narration for the ear, not headlines read aloud. Plan around 2 words/second and measured audio; never speed up speech to hide timing errors.
- Captions equal actual speech using real timestamps. Pipeline adds captions; do not duplicate.
- Voice, music, ambient/product sound and silence serve the story. Use supported capabilities and clear commercial rights; paid music needs workflow authorisation. Keep speech intelligible, duck music, avoid borrowed ringtones/notifications.
- Technical review covers renderer, timing, zones, files, coverage, copy, captions and facts. Creative review covers insight, hook/payoff, evidence, brand necessity, composition, type, motion, sound, pacing, action and diversity.
- Text/code checks cannot prove visual quality, playback or commercial effectiveness. Record actual inspection; never turn review errors into approval.
